On the Importance of Evaluating Foundational Time-Series Models One Step Ahead in an Investment Context

Peter Cotton · technical note, November 2026 · PDF · tex · bib

Popular benchmarks for foundational time-series models pool forecast errors over long horizons. Under frictionless log utility, an investor who can trade every interval in a liquid market does not use those horizons: the optimal position depends only on the conditional distribution of the next return, and the investor's expected log-wealth regret from trading on a forecast is bounded by the forecast's relative entropy from the truth. With transaction costs, in the quadratic model of Gârleanu and Pedersen, later horizons enter through the expected-return path with weights that shift toward the next interval as costs fall. The one-step predictive is therefore the object to evaluate for that investor, its log score controls the investor's regret without ranking imperfect forecasts' decisions, and a score pooled over horizons does not establish superiority for that decision. Two examples mark the limits: a model better at one step can be worse at twelve while iterating its own dynamics, and a forecast closer to the truth in relative entropy can induce the worse position. We recommend reporting one step ahead on its own and including an iterated one-step model among multi-step baselines.
