On the Importance of Evaluating Foundational Time-Series Models One Step Ahead in an Investment Context

Peter Cotton · technical note, November 2026 · PDF · tex · bib

Popular benchmarks for foundation time-series models pool forecast errors over long horizons. An investor who can trade every interval in a liquid market does not use those horizons. Under log utility the optimal position depends only on the conditional distribution of the next return; later horizons enter only through transaction costs, with weights that shrink as the market gets more liquid. One-step accuracy is therefore the quantity to evaluate for that investor, and a score pooled over horizons does not establish superiority for them. Two examples show that neither coherence across horizons nor a better proper score orders decisions, and one identity shows how a better one-step model improves a multi-step model's path forecasts. We recommend reporting one step ahead on its own and including an iterated one-step model among multi-step baselines.
