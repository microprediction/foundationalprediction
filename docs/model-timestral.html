Timestral

foundation · Mistral AI · 209M · licence: code Apache-2.0; weights Modified MIT · GitHub · Model card

Timestral is Mistral's zero-shot time-series model, released 15 October 2026 in four sizes; it emits 21 non-crossing quantiles per step, from the 1st to the 99th percentile.

How it was run

Run zero-shot on CPU with a fixed 128-length context, symmetric averaging and the nonnegativity clip switched off (the series are signed changes). The headline row is the 209M checkpoint scored on all 21 native levels; the other sizes and the nine-decile reconstruction are tabled below.

Timestral is a patched transformer with an hourglass encoder, trained to emit 21 quantile levels per step with a context of up to 32,768 points and support for covariates and multiple variates, none of which this univariate study uses. Its 1% and 99% quantiles are native outputs, so its density needs no bandwidth inflation to carry tails. Scored on all 21 levels, the 123M and 209M are ahead of Laplace at the median on weekly, monthly and M4-hourly series, level on price, and a few hundredths behind on daily econ, with the same coverage as Laplace. On the nine deciles alone it reads like every other quantile model, about half a nat behind. Pretraining contamination is unchecked.

Against laplace, one step ahead

Per series, Diebold–Mariano on the log score; a win is the model ahead beyond 1.96 standard errors, a loss laplace ahead, the rest draws. Median ΔLL is the model's log score minus laplace's, in nats per step; CRPS ratio is model over laplace (below 1 favours the model); coverage is the share of outcomes inside the central 90%. The methodology page has the protocol; the leaderboards place the model among the rest.
