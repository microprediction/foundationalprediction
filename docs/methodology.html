Methodology

How the leaderboards are scored. How laplace and laplace++ (granger) are built, and the transforms and ensembles underneath them, is in the skaters guide.

The question

Which model should forecast the next observation of a stream? That question needs the one-step metric isolated. Suites that pool steps 1 to H into one score cannot answer it, and a model that is strong at step one and ordinary after is indistinguishable there from one that is flat.

The protocol

Every model forecasts the one-step change of a series from a fixed context, rolling over a test window; every forecast becomes a full predictive density scored by log likelihood and CRPS at the realised value. Pretrained models run zero-shot, each in its own environment, on CPU. Their quantile outputs are turned into densities by a stated reconstruction: native levels where the model emits them (21 for Timestral and t0-gamma), the generous bandwidth otherwise, because a tight reconstruction of nine deciles manufactures about half a nat of apparent deficit.

Pairing and the test

Every model is paired against laplace, the training-free online yardstick, on identical series and windows. Per series the mean log-score differential is tested by Diebold–Mariano with a Newey–West standard error; a win or a loss is a difference beyond 1.96 standard errors, the rest are draws. A draw says the window cannot separate the two, not that they are equal, so the median gap is the better measure of size.

Baselines that keep learning

The classical baselines in published suites are fit once at the window origin and never updated. Here every baseline is online: it sees every observation before it forecasts the next. The gap between foundation models and classical methods shown here is therefore the gap a practitioner running a stream would see. AutoARIMA, AutoETS, AutoTheta and GARCH-t are refit at every step.

Trained models

laplace++ is the one trained model here. It is trained on half of each panel, split by series family, and every number shown for it is on the other half. Training data added from outside the panel is never one of the held-out series. How it is built and what it guarantees are on the laplace++ page.

Training-data overlap

A pretrained model may have seen the evaluation series, or earlier stretches of them, in training. The panels are FRED economic and price series and the M4 hourly set. What each model's own documentation says:

  • Chronos-2: pretraining includes all 414 M4 hourly series, the whole hourly panel here, and other M4 frequencies; no FRED dataset is named (report, Table 6).
  • Moirai 2.0: pretraining includes GIFT-Eval's pretraining set, which contains fred_md (107 FRED-MD monthly series), and GIFT-Eval's training split, which covers every M4 frequency (paper).
  • TTM-R3: trained on selected GIFT-Eval pretraining and training datasets; the list is not published (model card).
  • TiRex-2: pretrained on the Chronos datasets and LOTSA, which include M4 and the Monash archive's fred_md (model card).
  • TinyCast: GIFT-Eval's pretraining set with the datasets that overlap the GIFT-Eval and Chronos zero-shot test sets removed; the exclusion list is not published (paper).
  • Toto 2.0: Datadog observability metrics and synthetic series only, no public forecasting data (paper).
  • Timestral, t0-gamma, TimeH2O, TimesFM, Chronos-Bolt and TiRex: not yet checked against these panels.
  • laplace++: runs on the weekly, monthly and M4 hourly panels. It is trained on one half of each, split by series family, and scored on the other; the weekly run also trained on GIFT-Eval's weekly pretraining series (NN5, road and web traffic), none of them in the panels.

Where a model has seen a panel, its row on that panel measures recall as well as forecasting.
