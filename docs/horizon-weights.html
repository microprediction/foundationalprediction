Horizon weights

How much each forecast lead matters to a trader who pays to trade, approximately.

A frictionless log-optimal trader uses only the next interval's predictive distribution, which is why the leaderboard scores one step ahead (note). With trading costs, later leads enter today's trade. In the quadratic-cost model of Gârleanu and Pedersen (2013) the expected one-interval price change at lead j gets weight z(1−z)j−1. This page puts a number on z for common trading set-ups.

Bars: the weight on each lead. Dashed line: equal weight on each of 48 leads, which is how a score pooled over a 48-step window (GIFT-Eval's hourly setting) weights them. Hover a bar for its value.

Where the number comes from

Gârleanu and Pedersen's trader pays ½λσ2(Δn)2 to trade and has risk aversion γ. The weights depend on the cost only through λ/γ, and with no discounting z = γ/(γ + a) with λ/γ = (a/γ)(1 + a/γ).

Real costs are closer to linear: a one-way cost c per unit traded. We match the two at the trade the policy actually makes, a fraction a/λ of the Markowitz position. That gives a/γ = 2c/α, where α is the expected return per interval, taken here as IC times volatility. So the mean lead is 1 + 2c/α. In words: the leads that matter run out to about the time the signal's accumulated alpha takes to pay for a round trip.

What the weights are not

They are an approximation in three places.

  • The calibration from linear to quadratic cost is ours. The note deliberately stops at the model; this page adds a rough map from a trading set-up to it.
  • The weights are a trading policy for expected returns. The loss from forecast errors ej is proportional to (Σwjej)2, which has cross-lead terms. If the errors at different leads were independent, the importance of lead j would be wj2, which is even more concentrated on lead 1. Either way, a weighted sum of per-lead scores is an approximation to the decision loss, not the loss itself.
  • Lead j means the return over the single interval j steps ahead. A pooled benchmark scores the level at step j, which bundles every interval up to j.
